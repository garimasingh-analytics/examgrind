# ExamGrind public copy style

Write for a student who has opened the app between study sessions.

## Use

- Short, direct sentences.
- Familiar exam-prep words: topic, chapter, mistake, quiz, revise, practise, mock and study plan.
- Specific next actions: “Review this quiz”, “Practise this topic”, “Open the guide”.
- Plain descriptions of what a feature does before any brand language.

## Avoid

- Em-dash punchlines in headings and buttons.
- Formulaic contrasts such as “not X, but Y” or “not just X”.
- Abstract product words such as “signal”, “proof”, “evidence” and “recovery” when a plain student-facing word is clearer.
- Claims that sound more certain than the product can support.
- Invented urgency, exaggerated promises, or startup-style slogans.

## Check before publishing

1. Would a student say this out loud naturally?
2. Does it explain what happens after the tap?
3. Is the heading useful without reading the paragraph beneath it?
4. Is a contrast, dash or clever phrase actually needed?
